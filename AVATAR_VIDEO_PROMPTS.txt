================================================================================
AVATAR VIDEO PROMPTS — for Google Veo 3.1, image-to-video
================================================================================

WHY VIDEO INSTEAD OF MORE STILLS

  One clip gives hundreds of frames of the same face under the same light, with
  the same framing. That is worth more than any number of separately generated
  stills, because the thing that ruins a still set is identity drift between
  them — and within one clip there is none.

  It also unlocks the change queued in PROJECT_STATUS: with dense coverage the
  engine can stop *blending* sources and start *selecting the nearest* one,
  which removes cross-fade ghosting by construction.


BEFORE GENERATING ANYTHING — three things to confirm on the plan being paid for

  1. IMAGE-TO-VIDEO from the attached portrait.png. Text-only generation makes a
     different person and every photograph, threshold and mask already tuned to
     this face becomes worthless.
  2. NO VISIBLE WATERMARK at this tier. Not "removable" — absent. A mark across
     the face makes a frame useless, and removing one is a licence breach in
     exactly the commercial case this is for.
  3. COMMERCIAL USE, output owned by you.

  These are from the checklist in docs/development/PROJECT_STATUS.md. The first
  is the one most likely to be missed and the most expensive to get wrong.


WHAT MATTERS IN THE OUTPUT

  - The face LARGE in frame. Frames are cut from this; the current portrait is
    1024x1024 and a small face in a wide shot is a downgrade.
  - SLOW, DELIBERATE movement. A frame taken during a fast movement is motion
    blurred and useless. Every prompt below asks for unhurried motion and brief
    holds — that is deliberate, not decoration.
  - LOCKED CAMERA. Any camera move changes the framing between frames.
  - CONSTANT LIGHT. The engine relights the avatar to the room; a source that
    changes brightness mid-clip poisons that.
  - ONE CONTINUOUS TAKE. No cuts.

  Attach portrait.png to every one of these.


================================================================================
THE SHARED BLOCK — put this at the end of EVERY prompt below
================================================================================

Static locked-off camera at eye level, no camera movement, no zoom, no pan, no
cuts, one continuous take. Close-up head-and-shoulders framing with the face
filling most of the frame, centred, the whole head visible with a small margin
above the hair. Soft even frontal lighting that never changes. Plain uncluttered
light grey background. Photorealistic, sharp focus on the face, natural skin
texture, no film grain, no colour grading, no vignette, no depth-of-field blur
on the face. The person keeps exactly the identity, hairstyle, facial hair,
clothing and skin tone of the attached photograph. Hands stay out of frame and
nothing ever covers the face. No on-screen text, captions or subtitles. All
movements are slow and deliberate, with each expression held briefly before
changing.

CLIP 1 — SPEECH  (the most valuable one; generate this first)
================================================================================

The person in the attached photograph is speaking directly to the camera in a

unhurried pace, articulating clearly, so the mouth passes through the full range
of natural speech shapes — wide open vowels, rounded lips, closed consonants,
teeth showing and hidden. They pause briefly between sentences with the mouth
closed and relaxed. Their eyes stay on the camera lens throughout, with natural
blinking. Small, gentle head movements only.

CLIP 1 — READY TO PASTE, WITH THE SPOKEN SCRIPT
================================================================================

  Three versions of the same clip. Each is complete: copy one, attach
  portrait.png, send. Generate them in order — the first alone is worth more
  than the other two together.

  The words are not arbitrary. Each script is loaded with the sounds that make
  distinct mouth shapes, so a few seconds of speech produces frames the engine
  cannot otherwise get:

     m / b / p   lips fully closed        "may", "before", "problem"
     f / v       upper teeth on lip       "for", "five", "value"
     oo / oh     lips rounded forward     "you", "move", "show", "so"
     ee / ay     lips wide, teeth close   "team", "each", "need"
     aah         jaw open, teeth visible  "ask", "start", "large"
     s / sh / ch what the teeth look like "sure", "show", "much"

  He should sound like a sales manager on a first call: friendly, unhurried,
  confident. Slow delivery matters more than a natural pace — every frame taken
  mid-movement is blurred and wasted.

CLIP 4 — HEAD TURNS
================================================================================

The person in the attached photograph keeps a neutral expression with the mouth
closed and turns their head very slowly and smoothly from side to side: from
facing the camera, gradually to one side until they are almost in profile, then
slowly back through centre, then gradually to the other side until almost in
profile, then slowly back to centre. The movement is continuous and unhurried,
taking several seconds in each direction, with a brief pause at each extreme and
at centre. Their eyes stay on the camera lens the whole time, so the head turns
but the gaze does not. The head stays upright with no tilt.

CLIP 5 — HEAD TILTS AND GAZE
================================================================================

The person in the attached photograph keeps a neutral expression with the mouth
closed. First they slowly raise their chin and tilt the head back, hold, and
return to level; then slowly lower the chin and tilt the head forward, hold, and
return to level. Then, keeping the head still and level and facing the camera,

other side, back to the lens, upward, back, downward, and back to the lens. Each

================================================================================
CLIP 6 — MOUTH SHAPES
================================================================================

The person in the attached photograph looks at the camera and slowly forms a
series of distinct mouth shapes, holding each for about a second and returning
briefly to a closed relaxed mouth between them: lips pressed firmly together as
in "m", lips rounded and pushed forward as in "oo", lips stretched wide and flat
with the teeth close together as in "ee", the jaw dropped wide open as in "aah"
with the teeth and the dark interior of the mouth visible, the upper teeth
resting on the lower lip as in "f", and finally the corners of the mouth pulled
down in a frown. The head stays still and facing the camera, eyes on the lens.

IF ONLY ONE CLIP IS POSSIBLE
================================================================================

Generate CLIP 1. Natural speech alone passes through most of the mouth shapes,
blinks, and small head movements, and it is the case the avatar spends almost
all of its time in on a real call.

If two: add CLIP 4. Head rotation is the one thing stills have consistently
failed to cover well, and it is what breaks first when someone turns to look at
a second monitor.


================================================================================
WHEN THE CLIPS ARRIVE
================================================================================

Do not sort frames by hand. The plan already recorded in PROJECT_STATUS is:

  1. extract frames from the video;
  2. run every one through scripts/check_avatar_photos.py, which measures
     identity, framing, lighting and pose, so a clip where the face drifted is
     caught rather than trusted;
  3. classify each usable frame by its own blendshapes and head pose — the same
     measurements the engine already uses to choose a source;
  4. switch the engine from blending sources to selecting the nearest.

  Step 4 is the reason the video is worth generating. It needs dense coverage,
  which is exactly what a clip gives and a set of stills never will.

  Keep the original video files. Frames can always be re-extracted; a clip that
  has been deleted has to be paid for again.
